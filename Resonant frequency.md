- The resonance frequency of a system is the frequency at which the response amplitude to a defined input is a relative maximum compared to the response at other frequencies. 
- At resonance frequencies, small periodic driving forces may produce large amplitude oscillations due to the storage of vibrational energy. 
- The resonance frequency is calculated as f<sub>0</sub>=1/(2πXsqrt (IC)) where f<sub>0</sub> is the resonance frequency, C is the compliance and I is the inertance.
- Therefore, the lower the compliance, the higher the resonance frequency (and vice versa). Therefore, neonates with parenchymal lung disease e.g., HMD should be ventilated with a frequency in the range of 12-15 Hz
- Calculate for compliance of 0.002 L/cm H<sub>2</sub>O (normal is 0.004 to 0.006) and inertance of 0.025 cm H<sub>2</sub>O/sec<sup>2</sup>/L
- Once the ventilator pushed the gas at high flow to the trachea/central airways, the amount of pressure transmitted to the lungs and therefore the pressure that remains in the central airways is determined by the impedance of the respiratory system beyond the central airways (compliance, resistance, and inertance) See [[Can HFV cause barotrauma? - Pressure dampening]] 
- For a given combination of lung mechanics, as the frequency increases and reaches the resonant frequency, the impedance level reduces and more pressure is transmitted to the alveoli. The impedance is lowest at the level of resonant frequency when the amplitude pressure in the alveoli is equal to or even higher than the amplitude pressure in the central airways
- It implies that the amplitude pressure transmission to the alveoli is
	- Maximum at the level of resonant frequency
	- Increases again when the frequency is decreased
	- Therefore, to minimize the pressure cost of delivering any value of TV and to minimize the pressure transmission to the alveoli need to understand the concept of the [[Corner frequency]]
