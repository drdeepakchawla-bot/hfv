- All types of HFV move the transition point between convective flow and diffusion in a cephalad direction (from acini to larger airways)
- Conventional ventilation AV = TV – DV
	- HFV works even when TV<=DV
- Multiple modes of gas transport in all types of HFV:
	- Bulk convection (direct ventilation in close alveoli)
	- Turbulent flow and radiant mixing
	- Asymmetric velocity profile (due to the difference between inspiratory and expiratory velocity profiles)
	- Convective dispersion 
	- Taylor-type dispersion (this would be more important in HFJV where a pulse of fresh gas is injected into already moving gas in the airways. The pulse of the injected gas will move fast at the central and relatively slowly near the walls of the airway. Gradually there is a dispersion of the injected gas pulse in the airway).
	-  High-frequency pendelluft (movement of gas among alveoli with different time constants)
	- Laminar flow and radial mixing
	- Molecular diffusion
	- Collateral ventilation
	- Cardiogenic mixing
- Other points
	- Oscillatory pressure applied at the airway opening is damped by resistance and inertance of the tracheal tube and central airways
	- Proximal alveolar units are exposed to central airway oscillatory pressure
	- Atelectatic compartments exposed to increased oscillatory pressure
	- Expanded and aerated alveoli protected from high oscillatory pressures
	- High peripheral resistance increases pressure transmission to airways and nearby alveoli
	- Alveoli protected due to decreased flow resulting from increased peripheral resistance
