### Animal studies
HFV
- Works at lower distal oscillatory pressure than CMV
- Reduces VILI
- Reduces lung inflammation markers
- Improves gas exchange
- Synergistic with surfactant and iNO
- Reduces exposure to oxygen
### Human studies
==Many studies with HFV including the largest HIFI trial conducted in the pre-surfactant and pre-steroid era. Also- no lung protective ventilation or use of volume guarantee==
- An individual patient data meta-analysis published in 2010 included 10 trials (including HIFI and UKOS, the two largest HFV trials) and 3229 neonates. No difference was observed in the composite primary outcome of death or BPD  death or severe adverse neurological event or the components of the composite outcome.  No heterogeneity across studies or difference in subgroups based on gestation, gender, SGA, antenatal steroids, the timing of surfactant, OI at trial entry, timing from birth to randomization, the brand of the ventilator, and ventilation strategy was observed. HFV group showed a higher incidence of 'any' air leak and a trend of lower risk of ROP. (Cools F, Askie LM, Offringa M, Asselin JM, Calvert SA, Courtney SE, et al. Elective high-frequency oscillatory versus conventional ventilation in preterm infants: a systematic review and meta-analysis of individual patient’s data. Lancet. 2010;375(9731):2082–91.)
- A Cochrane review updated in 2015 included 19 studies (4096 neonates) and reached similar inclusion. Although a reduction in the incidence of CLD at 36 weeks was observed (RR: 0.86, 0.78 to 0.96), there was heterogeneity between the trials. (Cools F, Offringa M, Askie LM. Elective high-frequency oscillatory ventilation versus conventional ventilation for acute pulmonary dysfunction in preterm infants. Cochrane Db Syst Rev. 2015;(3):CD000104.)
- A meta-regression in 2007 attempted to evaluate the following factors associated with the clinical effect of HFOV: year of publication; use of Sensormedics 3100A ventilator for HFV; time on CMV prior to starting of study; gestational age; use of surfactant; high lung volume strategy in HFV; and lung protective ventilation strategy in CMV and baseline risk. Although none of these factors were found to be significant with 95% CI being wide, the most likely influential factors that favored good outcomes were the use of open lung volume strategy in HFV and of lung protective ventilation in conventional ventilation. (Bollen CW, Uiterwaal CSPM, Vught AJ van. Meta-regression analysis of high-frequency ventilation vs conventional ventilation in infant respiratory distress syndrome. Intensive Care Med. 2007;33(4):680–8.)
- Apart from the use of OLV, other factors that have been proposed to increase the probability of favorable outcomes with HFV include
	- Early use of HFOV (< 6 h)
	- I:E ration of 1:2
	- Extubation to CPAP rather than CMV
- Some studies (HIFI and Courtney) have shown a higher incidence of IVH or PVL or both - the reason can be wide fluctuation in pCO2 and accompanying higher incidence of air leaks
