## Homogenous lung disease
Examples: respiratory distress syndrome, diffuse pneumonia and bilateral lung hypoplasia
- As generally taught in HFOV including [[Lung recruitment maneuver]]
- If available use volume guarantee as this leads to
	- Automatic adjustment of amplitude as lung moves on the compliance curve with disease evolution
	- Automatic weaning
## Inhomogenous lung disease
Examples:  focal pneumonia, pulmonary hemorrhage, meconium aspiration, unilateral lung hypoplasia, and BPD
- **Goal**:  oxygenate and ventilate effectively, allowing injured areas to heal whilst simultaneously avoiding new injury to more healthy lung regions
- therapeutic challenge due to regional differences in compliance and/or resistance
- Condition 1: Reduction of regional inhomogeneities particularly if because of difference in compliance is best minimized by the use of higher frequencies > increased acceleration of the flow waveform > greater pressure losses in normally ventilated areas whilst simultaneously allowing pressure in atelectatic areas to increase to the threshold pressure at which recruitment occurs
- Condition 2: If regional inhomogeneities are due to increased resistance or air trapping - use of lower frequency and an I:E ratio of 1:2 > longer oscillatory cycle > adequate expiratory time. Maintain adequate MAP to prevent airway collapse and air trapping during active expiration 
- Follow the disease evolution e.g., MAS in the initial phase due to meconium in airways acts like condition 2 and later with the opening of airways and onset of chemical pneumonitis like condition 1
## Air leaks
Examples: pulmonary interstitial emphysema (PIE), bullous emphysema, pneumomediastinum, and pneumothoraces
- Be careful as studies have shown increased air leaks with HFOV. Need to avoid static and cyclic overdistension (that can occur due to transmission of oscillatory pressure) and to avoid air trapping
- Pneumothorax: Target low TV ventilation - higher frequency and avoid sigh breaths, lower MAP
- PIE: Lower frequency and short I:E ratio to promote lung emptying and lower MAP
- Continue HFV for 24-48 after resolution to prevent a recurrence
## PPHN
- Consider underlying cause
- Primary PPHN: treat hypovolaemia and hypotension prior to commencement of HFOV
- Select MAP to minimize PVR, usually not higher than CV is needed
- Frequency guided by patient size
- Useful to combine iNO with HFOV
