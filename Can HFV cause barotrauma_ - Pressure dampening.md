The extent of pressure changes at the airway opening caused by the set amplitude level is not transmitted fully to the airways or to the alveoli. There is a rapid dampening of the pressure excursions. This also leads to low tidal volumes in HFV. However, low TV does not necessarily mean low transmission of oscillatory pressure amplitudes.   
What determines the pressure dampening? - Primarily determined by **impedance** to the flow
- Impedance - a general mechanical term that describes the mechanical barrier to flow. Impedance incorporates the volume-dependent (elastic),  flow-dependent (resistive), and acceleration-dependent (inertive) properties of the respiratory system. (compliance = 1/Elastance).
	- **Compliance**: With lower lung compliance, higher pressure amplitudes are required to generate the same tidal volume, inspiratory and expiratory time constants are shorter and more percentage of pressure amplitude is transmitted from the airway opening to the alveoli ![[Screen Shot 2023-02-06 at 4.17.51 PM.png]]
		- The [[Oscillatory pressure ratio]] is lowest at the point of maximal compliance. 
	- **Resistance**: Increased resistance increases the impedance to the flow and therefore reduces the pressure amplitude at the alveoli. With higher resistance, a higher amplitude is required to achieve the same tidal volume. However, with higher amplitude, a higher percentage is transmitted to the airways proximal to the site of resistance. This can lead to barotrauma to proximal airways. 
		- Narrower ET and greater flow rates also lead to increased resistance and therefore need for a higher amplitude to achieve the same TV. 
	- **Inertance**: Inertance forces are needed when there is a change in direction of the movement - switch from inspiration to expiration of gases. As the frequency of change is higher, flow rates are higher and changes are more abrupt, inertance plays a more important role in HFV than in tidal breaths. 
- The presence of inhomogeneous lung areas i.e. areas with different compliance and resistance leads to a differential effect of the above factors. For example, collapsed or low-volume alveoli due to lower compliance will receive more transmission of the amplitude pressure. This may lead to an increased risk of barotrauma but also helps in lung recruitment. 

## Role of frequency in pressure dampening
- As HFV frequency increases, the dampening increases, and therefore less amplitude pressure waveform is transmitted to the alveoli. 
- Therefore, lower frequency leads to the higher amplitude pressure transmission 

Pressure needed = (Pressure needed per unit convective flow) X (convective flow needed per unit alveolar ventilation)
The second part (flow cost) is directly proportional to dead space ventilation which in turn is directly proportional to PEEP and frequency
