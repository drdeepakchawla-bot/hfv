- At frequency >5 Hz tidal volume has a greater impact on the minute ventilation
- MV ~ frequency X TV<sup>2</sup> 
- Therefore, even small changes in TV can result in large changes in pCO<sub>2</sub> 
- Therefore with HFV
	- The direct relationship between frequency and pCO<sub>2</sub> is not valid as a change in frequency causes a change in tidal volume. Hence, a more correct formula would be 
			MV ~ frequency<sup>(less than 1)</sup> X TV<sup>(more than 1)</sup> 
- ==However, the reverse effect of frequency as shown in the above formula does not hold true if volume guarantee is used in HFV.==

- Oxygenation is dependent on MAP and FiO<sub>2</sub>
- Ventilation is controlled by the adjustment of the delivered tidal volume. TV is controlled by amplitude, frequency, and percent inspiratory time.
However, oxygenation and ventilation are not truly independent in HFV.
- Change in MAP causes a change in lung compliance (where the lung is on the static compliance curve) and therefore influences the tidal volume that can be delivered to the lung
- Reduced frequency leads to more pressure transmitted to the lungs at the same set MAP and therefore may also affect the oxygenation
- The mean pressure in the airways and lungs and is lower than the MAP measured at the ventilator end. This drop in MAP is dependent on:
	- Flow
	- Frequency
	- I:E ratio
	- Amplitude
	- Tracheal diameter/size of ET
