Continue monitoring. Changes in pCO2 are very rapid. Changes affected by MAP are slow and take at least 15 minutes.
- **Oxygenation**: Bring FiO2 down to 25-30%. Then reduce MAP by 1-2 cmH2O every 1-4 hours. If needed recruitment can be done with a brief increase in MAP. Prevent overdistension. If overdistension not responding to a reduction in MAP - reduce the frequency or change the I:E ratio to give more expiratory time. 
- **Ventilation**: Maintain a clear airway and monitor DCO2
	- If suction is needed, follow it by recruitment - brief small increases in mean airway pressure (2-4 cmH2O ) or transient (5 min) increases in sigh frequency (max 2-5 breaths/min) or application of 2-3 manual sustained inflations of 2-3 s each. Sigh breath Ti should be longer (e.g., 0.5 s) and pressure smaller (e.g., 5 cm H2O above MAP)
	- To target the desired DCO2, better to change the TV by changing the amplitude. The effect of change in the frequency can be unexpected. Reducing frequency can also lead to more barotrauma (see [[Peak carinal pressure]])
- **Humidification**: Appropriate humidification is essential to prevent atelectasis of alveoli due to the blockage of small airways. Also, prevent condensation of fluid in the tubing - reduces TV delivery

### Weaning
- Preferred to non-invasive mode
- Reduce MAP to what can be given in NIV and amplitude so that the patient can breathe spontaneously
- The time of weaning depends on the underlying disease. Faster for HMD or PPHN while for BPD may take many days.
