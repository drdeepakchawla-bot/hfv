- Corner frequency represents the point (boundary) within the frequency response of any system, at which the energy flowing through the system starts to be reduced rather than passing completely through. 
- The Corner frequency is related to the compliance and resistance (R) of the system, expressed as fc=1/(2πRC). 
- In the overdamped neonatal lung (which is usually the case, fc is usually below the resonance frequency.
![[Screen Shot 2023-02-06 at 9.35.00 PM.png]]

- Targeting oscillatory frequency to the corner frequency of the lung  potentially achieves the minimum pressure cost per unit of oscillatory flow
- As the time constant reduces, the corner frequency increases
![[Screenshot 2023-02-07 at 9.56.33 AM.png]]
A 600g preterm infant with compliance of 0.1 mL/ cmH2O (severe HMD) and a resistance of 100 cmH2O/L/s has a time constant of 10 ms (put 0.01 second in the formula given above) and a corner frequency of 15.9 Hz.

- Neonates with RDS and non-compliant (short time-constant) atelectatic lungs have a higher corner frequency than infants with healthy, normally compliant lungs with relatively longer time constants. 
- Neonates with high airway resistance (long time-constant) will have an even lower corner frequency than the infant with healthy lungs
